## Outcome and non-negotiable boundaries

Replace the player-facing affinities **Esprit, Force, Marée, Flamme, Sylve, Ombre** with the immediately readable types **Psy, Combat, Eau, Feu, Plante, Ténèbres**. Use the two canonical directional triangles:

- `Eau → Feu → Plante → Eau`
- `Psy → Combat → Ténèbres → Psy`

An arrow means the attacker deals `×2`; the reverse attack deals `×0.5`; every cross-triangle pairing and same-type pairing deals `×1`.

Legal/art boundary:

- The six words are generic French elemental/category names. Do not use Pokémon logos, fonts, badge/card frames, color layouts, icon silhouettes, terminology such as “super type” copied as trade dress, or any other franchise asset.
- Draw six original, one-color, code-native SVG marks: asymmetric flame, notched water drop, offset-vein leaf, compact clenched fist, almond eye with an off-center spiral, and cutout crescent moon. Use only generic geometry and document the source as an original Arène de Noam asset.
- Do not trace, import, screenshot, recolor, or visually approximate Pokémon’s official type icons. The official type chart is a rules reference only.
- Keep “Arène de Noam” visual framing, arena emblems, typography, cards, and badges original. Familiarity comes from names, elemental colors, and relations—not copied presentation.

Canon check: the [official Pokémon type chart](https://sg.portal-pokemon.com/game/type-chart/) confirms the six directed advantages. The starter triangle is symmetric at `2×`/`0.5×`. The second triangle is directionally canonical—Psy beats Combat, Combat beats Ténèbres, Ténèbres beats Psy—but its reverse Psy→Ténèbres matchup is canonically **immune (`0×`)**, not merely resisted. Arène de Noam intentionally compresses that immunity to its existing `0.5×` vocabulary. Do not claim exact Pokémon rules; say “the familiar relations” and teach Arène de Noam’s two values explicitly.

## 1. Cycle decision

### 1.1 What a single six-cycle can and cannot preserve

The six desired canonical edges are two disjoint directed 3-cycles:

```text
Eau → Feu → Plante → Eau
Psy → Combat → Ténèbres → Psy
```

A single directed 6-cycle cannot contain either closed 3-cycle intact and still visit the other three types. It must break at least one edge in each triangle, keep two edges from each, and connect the two resulting paths with two false cross-triangle edges. Therefore:

- the mathematical maximum is **four of six canonical strong edges**;
- two canonical edges become neutral;
- two canonically neutral cross-triangle matchups become taught `×2` edges;
- because every strong edge also creates the inverse `×0.5` resistance, each false edge teaches two wrong expectations.

All maximum-preservation six-cycles, unique up to rotation, are below. “False” means canonically neutral but taught as `×2`; the reverse is also falsely taught as `×0.5`. “Missing” means a familiar canonical advantage is reduced to neutral.

| Maximum Option A order | False taught edges | Missing canonical edges |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eau → Feu → Plante → Psy → Combat → Ténèbres → Eau | Plante→Psy; Ténèbres→Eau | Plante→Eau; Ténèbres→Psy |
| Eau → Feu → Plante → Combat → Ténèbres → Psy → Eau | Plante→Combat; Psy→Eau | Plante→Eau; Psy→Combat |
| **Eau → Feu → Plante → Ténèbres → Psy → Combat → Eau** | **Plante→Ténèbres; Combat→Eau** | **Plante→Eau; Combat→Ténèbres** |
| Feu → Plante → Eau → Psy → Combat → Ténèbres → Feu | Eau→Psy; Ténèbres→Feu | Eau→Feu; Ténèbres→Psy |
| Feu → Plante → Eau → Combat → Ténèbres → Psy → Feu | Eau→Combat; Psy→Feu | Eau→Feu; Psy→Combat |
| Feu → Plante → Eau → Ténèbres → Psy → Combat → Feu | Eau→Ténèbres; Combat→Feu | Eau→Feu; Combat→Ténèbres |
| Plante → Eau → Feu → Psy → Combat → Ténèbres → Plante | Feu→Psy; Ténèbres→Plante | Feu→Plante; Ténèbres→Psy |
| Plante → Eau → Feu → Combat → Ténèbres → Psy → Plante | Feu→Combat; Psy→Plante | Feu→Plante; Psy→Combat |
| Plante → Eau → Feu → Ténèbres → Psy → Combat → Plante | Feu→Ténèbres; Combat→Plante | Feu→Plante; Combat→Ténèbres |

The bold row is the current internal order after a direct relabel, rotated to start at Eau:

```text
mind → force → tide → flame → grove → shadow → mind
Psy  → Combat→ Eau  → Feu   → Plante→ Ténèbres→ Psy
```

It already achieves Option A’s maximum four canonical edges: Psy→Combat, Eau→Feu, Feu→Plante, and Ténèbres→Psy. Its cost is particularly bad for the audience: it says Combat beats Eau and Plante beats Ténèbres, while the expected Plante→Eau and Combat→Ténèbres are merely neutral. A child who arrives with the correct Pokémon model is punished for transferring it, then taught two facts that become wrong again when returning to Pokémon. An arbitrary strong edge is more confusing than a neutral omission because the UI, forecasts, AI switching, and `×2` impact callout all reinforce it.

There is no more intuitive reordering hidden in Option A. Every row preserves four, omits two, and invents two. Choosing “plausible fantasy” false edges such as Plante→Ténèbres only makes the invented rule easier to memorize; it does not make it compatible with the audience’s existing knowledge.

### 1.2 Option B: two independent canonical triangles

Use exactly these relations:

```text
Eau ×2 Feu      Feu ×2 Plante      Plante ×2 Eau
Psy ×2 Combat   Combat ×2 Ténèbres Ténèbres ×2 Psy
```

The reverse of each listed relation is `×0.5`. All other pairs are `×1`.

Mechanical assessment:

- **No numeric richness is lost.** Before and after, every type has exactly one `×2` target, one `×0.5` attacker, and four neutral defenders when same-type is included. With four creatures in each type, uniform random pairings remain `1/6` strong, `1/6` resisted, and `4/6` neutral.
- The graph becomes two connected components instead of one six-node ring. That removes the two arbitrary bridges, so a team living entirely in one triangle has no advantage or weakness against the other triangle. This is a real loss of cross-triangle counterplay, but it is not a loss of edge count or matchup frequency.
- A complete three-type triangle covers every type inside that triangle and is neutral outside it. A mixed trio can cover both triangles; the existing “three different types” collection/feat logic remains meaningful even though it does not guarantee three distinct strong targets.
- Specialist rival teams become easier to scout from prior knowledge: Plante counters Eau, Combat counters Ténèbres, Psy counters Combat, and so on. Cross-triangle encounters become safe neutral teaching space rather than surprise punishment.
- The AI already asks `affinityMultiplier()` for move and switch scores. It needs no new heuristic or triangle awareness; its behavior automatically follows the new chart.
- Academy teaching becomes “two triangles you already know,” with one multiplier rule, one neutral cross-rule, and no exception list.

### 1.3 Recommendation

**Choose Option B with conviction.** The product goal is not to preserve the topology of an invented ring; it is to make the types instantly legible to a 12-year-old Pokémon player. Option B preserves all six familiar directional advantages, invents none, retains the same strong/weak/neutral distribution, and reduces Academy teaching. The disconnected graph is an acceptable and visible simplification. Do not keep a false edge merely to make one ring drawable.

The only declared approximation is Ténèbres resisting Psy at `×0.5` instead of Pokémon’s immunity. That is preferable to introducing a third multiplier solely for borrowed fidelity and is consistent with the settled Arène de Noam `2.0`/`0.5` system.

## 4. Balance and behavior expectations

### 4.1 Exact chart delta

Only four unordered pairings change:

| Pair | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combat (`force`) vs Eau (`tide`) | Combat `×2`, Eau `×0.5` | both `×1` |
| Plante (`grove`) vs Ténèbres (`shadow`) | Plante `×2`, Ténèbres `×0.5` | both `×1` |
| Plante (`grove`) vs Eau (`tide`) | both `×1` | Plante `×2`, Eau `×0.5` |
| Combat (`force`) vs Ténèbres (`shadow`) | both `×1` | Combat `×2`, Ténèbres `×0.5` |

Psy→Combat, Eau→Feu, Feu→Plante, and Ténèbres→Psy stay exactly as they are. The tutorial’s damage, first effectiveness callout, and existing Psy-resistant Perfect Relay fixtures should therefore remain stable aside from names/icons.

### 4.2 Predicted simulation impact

Global matchup frequencies and each type’s strong/weak count are invariant, so average pacing, neutral/super/resisted TTK bucket sizes, and naive win rates should move less than they would after a multiplier or stat change. However, this is **not** balance-neutral because the counter targets are reassigned between non-identical kits:

- Combat loses its edge over the bulky/sustaining Eau group and gains an edge over the generally faster, lower-HP Ténèbres group. Ferrax/Kordane into fragile Ténèbres targets is the clearest new super-effective one-shot risk.
- Plante loses its edge over Ténèbres and gains it over the bulky Eau group. Lower-pressure Plante sustain kits may need more hits even at `×2`, while they no longer suppress shadow assassins.
- Eau may rise against Combat but fall against Plante; Ténèbres may rise against Plante but fall against Combat. Individual creature rates can rotate even when type-level totals cancel.
- Smart lead, scout labels, remixed teams, Champion switches, predicted resistance, and Perfect Relay availability all update through the shared multiplier. Seeded AI decisions can therefore change far beyond the four active pairings.
- The regular-move super-effective full-HP one-shot rate is the first metric to watch. Do not weaken the canonical chart or change `2.0`/`0.5` to repair an outlier; identify the creature/move correlation first.

Expected outcome: existing broad gates should pass without tuning, but do not promise byte-identical simulation output. If a gate fails, compare same-seed before/after matrices, isolate Combat→Ténèbres and Plante→Eau rows, and make the smallest creature/move adjustment only after confirming the chart implementation is correct. No balance edit belongs in the initial chart commit.
